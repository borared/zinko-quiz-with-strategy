## 1. UX Research Conducted (Interviews)

**Goal:** Identify the problems faced by high school students during their studies.
**Main Question:** What is the problem that you face in high school?

**Interviewee 1: Bora Yorn (NPIC student)**
*Insights:* When I was in high school, I felt that my classmates spend their time in the class doing useless things, most of them don’t interact with each other. In my free time most of my guy friends played mobile games, they didn’t focus on review lessons. When the teacher asked about the last lesson, most of them didn’t perform well at all.

**Interviewee 2: Vandet Phat (RUPP Student)**
*Insights:* When I was in high school, I remember that I found out my whole class fell apart and some students played mobile games, some slept, some mind their own business in silence and that was really stressful for me. I really want to see my class feel connected, motivated.

## 2. User Pain Points Analyzed

Based on the interviews conducted, the following pain points have been identified for high school students:
1. **Lack of Engagement & Motivation:** Students find traditional lesson reviews boring, leading to distractions like sleeping or playing mobile games.
2. **Poor Class Connection (Isolation):** Students often mind their own business in silence or form isolated groups, resulting in a lack of classroom unity and peer-to-peer interaction.
3. **High Pressure / Stress:** The current environment feels stressful and unsupportive, making students dread answering teacher questions about past lessons.
4. **Ineffective Review Methods:** Students fail to retain knowledge from previous lessons because they do not have an engaging way to practice and review together.

## 3. Persona Created

### Persona 1: The Disengaged Gamer
**Name:** Rithy
**Age:** 16
**Occupation:** High School Student (Grade 10)

**Background:** 
Rithy is a smart kid but easily bored. During free time in class, he prefers playing mobile games with a small group of friends. He struggles to remember what the teacher taught the day before.

**Goals:**
- Wants to have fun and feel the thrill of competition.
- Wants a stress-free way to remember lessons without feeling like it's "homework."
- Wants to connect with his classmates in a way that feels natural to him.

**Frustrations:**
- Traditional studying feels tedious and lonely.
- Feels embarrassed when called upon by the teacher and doesn't know the answer.
- Hates the silent, awkward atmosphere in the classroom.

**How Zinko Helps:** 
Zinko turns reviewing lessons into a competitive, game-like experience. It uses the mechanics he loves from mobile games (strategy, hype, teamwork) to help him learn while interacting with the entire class.

## 4. User Stories Defined for Main Features

**Feature 1: Interactive Team-Based Quizzes**
- *As a student*, I want to join a team with my classmates so that we can collaborate and compete together.
- *As a student*, I want to answer quiz questions in a game-like format so that reviewing lessons feels like playing a fun game rather than taking a test.

**Feature 2: Real-time Leaderboard & Hype System**
- *As a student*, I want to see my team's score go up in real-time so that I feel motivated and excited to answer more questions correctly.
- *As a student*, I want to use special "strategies" or power-ups during the quiz so that the game feels unpredictable and thrilling.

**Feature 3: Low-Pressure Environment**
- *As a student*, I want to be able to make mistakes without being penalized individually so that I don't feel stressed about getting the wrong answer.

**Feature 4: Teacher / Host Dashboard**
- *As a teacher*, I want to easily launch a Zinko session for the class so that I can get everyone engaged and reviewing the previous day's lesson in 10 minutes.

## 5. User Flow / Journey Documented

**Scenario:** Rithy is in class, and the teacher wants to review yesterday's lesson using Zinko.

1. **Onboarding & Joining:**
   - The teacher starts a Zinko session on the smartboard/projector.
   - A QR code or Game PIN is displayed.
   - Rithy pulls out his phone (or tablet/laptop), goes to the Zinko website, and enters the Game PIN.
   - Rithy is randomly assigned to a team (e.g., "Red Team") with other classmates he normally doesn't talk to.

2. **The Hype Build-up (Lobby):**
   - The lobby screen shows all teams filling up. Avatars and funny team names pop up, making the class laugh and interact.
   - Rithy strategizes with his new teammates on how to win.

3. **Engagement (The Quiz Phase):**
   - The game starts. A question about yesterday's lesson appears on the main screen.
   - Rithy and his team discuss the answer quickly and submit it via their devices.
   - Correct answers earn points, but they can also choose to activate a "Strategy/Power-up" (e.g., doubling points, freezing another team) to make it more competitive.

4. **Climax & Connection:**
   - The leaderboard updates dynamically with hype animations.
   - Rithy's team takes the lead, and he high-fives his teammates. The previously silent classroom is now loud, energetic, and connected.

5. **Resolution:**
   - The game ends, and the winning team is celebrated on the screen.
   - Rithy realizes he just reviewed the whole lesson without feeling any pressure or stress. He actually looks forward to the next review session.
