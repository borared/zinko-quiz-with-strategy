# HCI Midterm Assignment: User Research and Design Brief
**Project:** Zinko - Interactive Strategy Quiz Game

---

## 1. Problem Statement
High school classrooms often suffer from low student engagement and peer isolation, particularly during lesson reviews. Traditional review methods and basic educational games often feel repetitive or individually isolating. As a result, students become distracted (e.g., playing mobile games or sleeping), fail to retain knowledge, and experience stress when called upon, leading to a disconnected classroom environment.

## 2. Target User
- **Primary:** High School Students (Aged 15–18) who struggle with engagement and motivation during classroom reviews.
- **Secondary:** High School Teachers/Lecturers looking for effective, cooperative tools to unify their classrooms and assess knowledge without causing student anxiety.

---

## 3. Short Interviews & Observations

**Interview 1: Bora Yorn (NPIC student reflecting on high school)**
*Observation/Quote:* "When I was in high school, I felt that my classmates spent their time in the class doing useless things, most of them didn’t interact with each other. In my free time most of my guy friends played mobile games, they didn’t focus on review lessons. When the teacher asked about the last lesson, most of them didn’t perform well at all."

**Interview 2: Vandet Phat (RUPP Student reflecting on high school)**
*Observation/Quote:* "I remember that I found out my whole class fell apart and some students played mobile games, some slept, some mind their own business in silence and that was really stressful for me. I really want to see my class feel connected, motivated."

---

## 4. Key Insights
1. **Lack of Engagement & Motivation:** Students find traditional lesson reviews boring, leading to distractions like sleeping or retreating into mobile games.
2. **Poor Class Connection (Isolation):** The classroom environment often fractures into isolated groups or silent individuals, resulting in a lack of peer-to-peer interaction and unity.
3. **High Pressure / Stress:** The current educational environment feels stressful. Students dread being singled out to answer questions about past lessons, which fosters anxiety rather than learning.
4. **Desire for Collaboration:** Students *want* to feel connected and motivated alongside their peers, pointing to a need for cooperative, team-based activities rather than purely individual competition.

---

## 5. Persona

**Name:** Rithy  
**Age:** 16 | **Occupation:** High School Student (Grade 10)  
**Archetype:** The Disengaged Gamer  

**Background:** 
Rithy is a smart kid but easily bored. During free time in class, he prefers playing mobile games with a small group of friends. Because he tunes out during traditional lectures, he struggles to remember what the teacher taught the day before.

**Goals:**
- Wants to have fun and feel the thrill of competition in a format he understands (gaming).
- Wants a stress-free way to remember lessons without feeling like it's tedious "homework."
- Wants to connect with his classmates in a way that feels natural and unforced.

**Frustrations:**
- Traditional studying feels tedious and lonely.
- Feels embarrassed when called upon by the teacher and doesn't know the answer.
- Hates the silent, awkward, and disconnected atmosphere in the classroom.

---

## 6. Journey Map

**Scenario:** Rithy is in class, and the teacher wants to review yesterday's lesson.

| Stage | Actions & Touchpoints | Emotional State |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Onboarding & Joining** | The teacher starts a Zinko session on the smartboard. A QR code/PIN is displayed. Rithy uses his phone to join and is assigned a random avatar and placed on a team with classmates he rarely talks to. | *Curious but slightly apprehensive.* |
| **2. The Hype Build-up (Lobby)** | The lobby screen shows teams filling up. Avatars and funny team names pop up on the big screen. Rithy strategizes with his new teammates on how to win. | *Excited, laughing, breaking the ice.* |
| **3. Engagement (Quiz Phase)** | A question about yesterday's lesson appears. Rithy and his team discuss the answer quickly and submit it via their devices. They use a "Strategy/Skill" (e.g., freezing the enemy team) to gain an advantage. | *Highly engaged, competitive, cooperative.* |
| **4. Climax & Connection** | The leaderboard updates dynamically with hype animations. Rithy's team cheers as they gain points together. Nobody is singled out for a wrong answer. | *Joyful, connected to peers, motivated.* |

---

## 7. Clear Opportunity Statement
**How might we** transform classroom lesson reviews into an interactive, team-based competitive game that leverages strategy mechanics (like those found in mobile gaming) to increase student engagement, reduce individual anxiety, and foster meaningful peer connection?
